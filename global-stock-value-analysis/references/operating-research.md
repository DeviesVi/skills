# Operating Research From Public Information

Use for substantive company analysis and for questions about business operations, operating assumptions, competitive advantages, or the feasibility of an earnings or cash-flow scenario. Reuse verified work in follow-ups. Choose the modules relevant to the business; a bank does not need a factory checklist, and a one-metric question does not require a new investment memo.

## Start With The Business And The Decision

Map the economic engine: what is sold, who pays, why customers choose it, how it is delivered, how pricing works, and what spending is required to remain competitive. Separate material segments, geographies, subsidiaries, and joint ventures when their economics or ownership differ.

Turn the investment question into operating questions that could change the answer. For example, a margin recovery may depend on pricing, yield, utilization, input costs, product mix, or accounting charges; a growth thesis may depend on customer conversion, distribution, procurement budgets, or regulatory approval. Identify the dominant drivers and plausible failure mechanisms before choosing valuation inputs.

For each material question, determine what observable evidence would support or disprove it and which public sources could provide that evidence. Financial results should reconcile the operating account, not substitute for it.

## Search Broadly, Then Follow The Important Gaps

Use company and product names, subsidiary legal names, project/site names, former names, competitors, and local-language terms as relevant. Search in the languages of the business and its principal markets when that can uncover original disclosures. Follow useful citations to the underlying document and check for subsequent updates.

Look beyond the latest reporting period when reconstructing an installation, contract, acquisition, or regulatory history. Compare earlier promises with actual outcomes. An older permit or prospectus can establish a project's identity or original design; it cannot establish today's utilization or remaining spending without an update.

The following are source options, not quotas or a requirement to retrieve every category:

| Operating question | Public information to investigate | What it can and cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Products, pricing, differentiation | Official product documentation, price lists, release history, service terms, technical specifications, demos, published procurement specifications | Compare actual functionality and commercial terms; a feature, patent, or launch does not establish adoption or pricing power |
| Customers, demand, retention | Customer filings and calls, public contracts and tenders, renewals, disclosed cohorts, usage metrics, cancellations, customer case studies | Separate evaluation, qualification, design wins, committed orders, delivery, revenue, and collection; logos and pilots do not prove material sales |
| Suppliers and bottlenecks | Supplier filings, equipment-maker orders and shipments, input-price data, sourcing announcements, trade or customs statistics | Check whose business and geography a signal covers; supplier orders may span customers or periods and are not automatically the target's production |
| Capacity and project execution | Project announcements, bond prospectuses, credit-rating reports, environmental assessments, permits, construction and equipment tenders, commissioning or acceptance records | Separate approved/nameplate capacity from installed, qualified, utilized, and saleable output; permits and ceremonies are not proof of full commercial operation |
| Market structure and competition | Competitor filings and product terms, industry associations, official statistics, credible specialist research, distributors' public disclosures | Align market definitions, units, time periods, and value versus volume share; issuer market-share claims need scrutiny |
| Channel economics and customer experience | Public distributor data, store or platform pricing, traffic and usage disclosures, product reviews, reliability reports, service complaints | Account for channel inventory, promotions, sampling bias, and retail versus manufacturer prices; anecdotes guide questions rather than prove a trend |
| Technical progress and execution capacity | Certifications, regulator approvals, standards participation, patents, public technical papers, hiring and organizational changes | Distinguish technical feasibility from economic production and commercial scale; hiring or patent counts alone are weak evidence |
| Management, incentives, capital allocation | Historical guidance, remuneration and incentive plans, insider ownership, related-party disclosures, deal documents, litigation and regulatory records | Test delivery against promises, acquisition returns, minority treatment, and use of cash rather than relying on stated strategy |

Company sources also include investor-day material, earnings-call Q&A, investor relations activity records, exchange Q&A platforms, and relevant prospectuses or transaction valuation reports. Customers, suppliers, and competitors can supply primary evidence about their own operations, but their observations may not represent the target's whole business.

Use reputable media and specialist research to find leads, context, and independently sourced observations. Identify the original origin of material claims. If only a reprint or search excerpt is accessible, state that limitation for a decisive claim. Do not present a summary as if the underlying document was inspected. Anonymous claims, forums, reviews, and promotional posts require corroboration before driving a conclusion. Access only information available through authorized public sources; research does not authorize contacting companies or other people.

## Keep An Evidence Record

For detailed research, keep a compact working record of claims that drive the conclusion. It can stay in working notes; do not force a separate artifact or large evidence table into every answer.

| Question / driver | Finding, unit and entity scope | Event date / covered period; publication date | Original source and location | Evidence status | Counterevidence, conflict or missing input | Earnings / cash-flow implication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Useful statuses are disclosed result, management guidance, third-party estimate, analyst calculation, analytical assumption, and unverified. A management forecast remains guidance even when repeated in several outlets. Distinguish the reliability of the source from the uncertainty of the event being forecast.

Reconcile contradictions by checking dates, definitions, units, ownership, scope, and the distinction between planned and actual events. A newer forecast does not erase an earlier missed commitment; preserve the execution history when it affects credibility. Multiple pages quoting one release are one origin, not independent corroboration. Unexplained conflicts lower confidence instead of being averaged away.

Search for disconfirming evidence on the central thesis: lost customers, delayed qualifications, cancellations, price cuts, excess inventory, utilization reductions, competitor capacity, technical substitution, and regulatory obstacles. Explain material missing data rather than treating absence of a disclosure as evidence that a problem or cost does not exist.

## Adapt The Operating Model

Investigate the drivers relevant to the company, including drivers omitted from standard financial summaries:

| Business type | Examples of operating drivers and distinctions |
| --- | --- |
| Manufacturing / semiconductors / industrials | Installed versus effective capacity, ramp schedule, yield, utilization, bottleneck tools, product mix, shipment volume, realized prices, customer qualification, input and energy costs, useful asset life |
| Software / platforms / transaction services | Paying versus registered users, seats or transaction volume, pricing and take rate, churn, gross versus net retention, contract duration, implementation burden, switching evidence, cloud/inference or processing cost |
| Consumer / retail / distribution | Same-store volume versus price growth, mature-store economics, openings and closures, channel inventory, sell-in versus sell-through, promotions, brand strength, franchise versus owned-store obligations |
| Resources / energy / infrastructure | Reserves and decline rates, recovery or load factors, realized versus benchmark prices, contracted versus merchant exposure, sustaining and decommissioning spending, concession life |
| Healthcare / regulated products | Approval versus reimbursement and adoption, trial or clinical evidence, payer/customer economics, exclusivity and substitutes, production readiness, commercial launch spending |
| Financials / insurers | Funding mix and costs, underwriting or credit quality, customer retention, capital and liquidity constraints, asset/liability duration, distributable capital rather than industrial capex or EBITDA |

Evaluate competitive advantages with observable behavior: renewal and switching events, procurement requirements, time to qualify substitutes, realized pricing, cost per comparable unit, share stability, and returns on incremental investment. Scale, customer lists, claimed technology leadership, and high revenue growth do not establish a moat by themselves.

## Capital-Intensive Businesses: Assets, Depreciation And Reinvestment

Use this module when asset commissioning, depreciation expiry, or capital expenditure materially affects the thesis. Do not infer a complete plant schedule from a headline launch year or total project budget.

For material facilities or asset cohorts, assemble the publicly supportable parts of: legal entity and parent ownership, location and technology, project phases, planned and installed capacity, commercial ramp, transfer into fixed assets, depreciable asset categories and cost, useful lives and residuals, impairment or later upgrades, remaining commitments, and expiry guidance. Label missing fields instead of filling a complete-looking schedule with guesses.

Keep these distinctions explicit:

- Announcement, construction start, equipment installation, trial production, qualification, commercial launch, accounting transfer, and full utilization are different events. Assets can enter depreciation in separate batches.
- Equipment, buildings, land rights, and capitalized development can have different accounting lives. Total project investment is not the depreciable equipment base. A group's disclosed range of asset lives is not a verified life for each plant.
- Initial equipment reaching the end of its accounting life does not end depreciation on later upgrades, replacement assets, or buildings. Accounting life, physical service life, and economic competitiveness can differ.
- Compare depreciation charges using the same note and scope across periods. Fixed-asset depreciation, depreciation plus amortization, cash-flow addbacks, and changes in net asset balances are not interchangeable.
- Reconcile gross depreciation expiry with new assets and upgrades entering depreciation. Do not attribute a consolidated decline to specific plants without a disclosed or defensible allocation.
- Construction-in-progress balances are already recognized assets, not an amount of future cash spending. Track unpaid commitments and future project phases separately; transfer to fixed assets does not itself create another equal cash outflow.
- Assess maintenance, periodic replacement, product upgrades, environmental requirements, and growth spending. A temporary pause in expansion is not proof of a permanently low reinvestment requirement.

Where the data support it, show a staged bridge from the reported baseline through old-asset expiry, new-asset charges, inventory recognition, related subsidy amortization, tax, and ownership changes. State the amount and period that each adjustment actually affects. Do not mechanically double a half-year decline or add a benefit already included in the starting earnings period.

## Connect Operations To Earnings And Shareholder Cash

Build the simplest model that captures the dominant economics. Examples include saleable units times realized price and mix; customers or transactions times net revenue per unit; or utilization times available capacity with the appropriate yield definition. Avoid multiplying overlapping adjustments such as utilization already measured on good output and a second yield discount.

Explain which operating observation supports each material assumption, what has to change from the current baseline, how long the change takes, and the uncertainty range. Reconcile the resulting revenue, margins, working capital, and reinvestment to reported segments and consolidated financials. Apply ownership and financing consistently before calling cash flow attributable to common shareholders.

Important cash-flow checks:

- Falling depreciation can raise accounting profit without increasing pre-tax operating cash flow by the same amount. In an indirect cash-flow bridge the lower addback offsets that profit change; consider actual cash-tax effects separately.
- Use one coherent earnings-to-cash or cash-flow-statement bridge. Do not deduct working-capital changes, interest, lease payments, or capitalized costs twice. Check their classification in the issuer's statements.
- Consolidated operating cash flow less capex is not automatically parent free cash flow. Consider financing, restricted or trapped cash, minority economic interests, lease obligations, and other material claims. Current minority dividends alone do not measure all cash economically owed to minorities.
- A larger parent stake acquired through buyouts or subsidiary capital reductions can improve future participation but requires a funding outlay. Include the cost and effective timing instead of treating ownership gains as free earnings.
- Distinguish distributable cash, cash retained for operations or investment, dividends, cancellation buybacks, and repurchases for employee incentives. Do not count both a buyback cash distribution and the same assumed share-count reduction as separate value gains.
- A terminal cash-flow assumption must fund periodic asset renewal and continued competitive relevance. A finite depreciation or expansion-spending window cannot be capitalized as perpetual growth without supporting economics.

## Test Whether A Forecast Can Be Achieved

For questions such as "can it reach this profit/cash-flow target?", turn the target into operating requirements and investigate them. Check demand and contract conversion, effective capacity and ramp timing, achievable prices and costs, required capital and working capital, and the ownership of the cash generated. Seek relevant historical outcomes and comparable projects with compatible definitions.

An achievable case needs both numerical consistency and evidence that the company can execute the required changes. Distinguish a favorable single year from through-cycle earning power. A table of assumed cash flows and valuations is sensitivity analysis, not evidence that one row is likely. Do not select a base case, confidence percentage, or fair-value midpoint merely because it is the middle of a table.

If decisive inputs remain unavailable after investigating relevant public channels, provide the supported bounds or reverse-valuation thresholds, identify exactly what is unverified, and explain which future disclosure could settle it. Give a limited feasibility judgment grounded in the evidence; do not replace research with another unsupported numerical scenario.

## Finish With Coverage And A Judgment

Before finishing substantive research, check that the important operating mechanisms have evidence, that the main thesis has been challenged, and that the financial/valuation implications follow from those findings. For a deep memo, briefly state the relevant source families inspected and material inaccessible or undisclosed inputs. Broad public research is a coverage goal, not a claim that every public item was collected.

Continue while a credible, accessible source type is likely to resolve a material uncertainty. Stop when further searches mostly repeat the same origin, remaining inputs are not publicly disclosed or accessible, or new information is unlikely to change the decision. Do not use this stopping rule to skip a relevant source family simply because a financial summary is available. Report access limitations and the actual scope of the research.

Lead the answer with the supported judgment, then the operating explanation, financial bridge, counterevidence, and unresolved conditions. Keep the reading length appropriate to the user's request even when the underlying research is extensive.
