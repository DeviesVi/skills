# News And Event Analysis

News matters only when it changes business value, risk, timing, or market perception enough to affect the investment decision. Use this reference when the user asks for recent news, catalysts, risk events, earnings reactions, or a current investment view.

## Source Priority

Prefer:
- company filings, exchange announcements, press releases, earnings calls, investor presentations
- regulator or court documents
- reputable financial media with clear sourcing
- industry data from recognized research bodies

Use caution with:
- anonymous source articles
- social media posts
- promotional newsletters
- single-source rumors
- price-action-only explanations

## Event Classification

Classify each item:

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thesis-changing | Alters normalized earnings power or moat | product failure, major regulation, durable margin reset |
| Risk signal | Raises downside probability | investigation, debt stress, auditor resignation |
| Catalyst | May unlock or accelerate value | buyback, spin-off, rate cut, approval, restructuring |
| Operating data point | Updates trend but needs context | monthly sales, order book, user growth |
| Sentiment/noise | Moves price without clear value change | short-term rumor, vague analyst note, broad market rotation |

## News Recency Rules

Use a default 30-90 day news window for current investment views. For earnings reactions, regulatory actions, credit stress, litigation, product launches, or price-sensitive events, check the last 1-7 days first, then widen the window only for context.

When the user asks for "today", "latest", "刚刚", "现在", or an event reaction, include retrieval time and distinguish live/updated articles from older background pieces. Primary company and regulator updates override media summaries.

## News Triage Workflow

1. List the 3-7 most relevant recent items with source, publication date, and retrieval date when the item is time-sensitive.
2. For each item, state the confirmed fact in one sentence.
3. Estimate what part of the valuation model it could affect: revenue, margin, capex, discount rate, terminal growth, debt, dilution, or multiple.
4. Mark impact as positive, negative, mixed, or unclear.
5. Mark durability as temporary, medium-term, or structural.
6. Decide whether the investment thesis changes.
7. Check whether any later announcement supersedes or corrects the item.

## Earnings And Guidance

For earnings news:
- compare reported revenue, EPS, margin, FCF, and guidance to prior trend and consensus when available
- identify one-off items and management adjustments
- check whether cash flow supports earnings
- note inventory, receivables, deferred revenue, backlog, and order trends when relevant
- separate a price reaction from the actual business update

## Macro And Policy News

Consider macro only through company-specific transmission:
- interest rates: discount rate, financing cost, demand, bank margins
- inflation: input costs, pricing power, real consumer demand
- FX: translation, transaction exposure, competitive position
- regulation: market access, margins, compliance costs, capital requirements
- geopolitics: supply chain, export controls, sanctions, customer demand

Do not overfit a company thesis to macro headlines unless the business has direct sensitivity.

## Output Pattern

Use this compact table when news is important:

| Date/time | News/Event | Source quality | Investment impact | Thesis effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  | Primary / reputable / unconfirmed | Positive / negative / mixed / unclear | Changes thesis / catalyst / risk / noise |

End with one paragraph: what news matters most, what is probably noise, and what to monitor next.
